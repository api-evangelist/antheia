The closure of the Strait of Hormuz by Iran is usually discussed in the language of geopolitics and energy markets. It is a chokepoint for oil, a pressure valve for global trade, and a lever of military strategy. But beneath those familiar headlines lies a less visible system of dependency — one that connects a naval blockade in the Middle East to pharmacy shelves.
